# Shelter and Lighting

A good early shelter is less about appearance than about controlling entrances, visibility, and storage. Build near the resources you use most often, but leave room to expand. A compact rectangle with a door, a roof, and a few windows is safer than a sprawling house with dark corners and several unprotected entrances.

## Build for Control

Place the bed, crafting table, furnace, and a few chests where they can be reached without crossing the entire room. Keep valuable tools and food together so an unexpected death does not scatter every important item across unrelated corners. A second exit is useful once the first base becomes a work site, but both exits should lead to lit paths.

Light is a defensive system. Hostile mobs spawn in dark areas, including spaces behind decorative blocks and beneath overhangs. Walk the perimeter after placing torches and look back toward the base from outside; any deep shadow is a place worth checking. Torches along a path also make navigation easier when returning from a mine after sunset.

### Expansion Without Regret

Leave a marked area for a crop plot and a small animal pen. [[food-and-farming|Food and farming]] explains why these should be close enough to maintain but far enough from the doorway that animals do not clog the entrance. Put the mine entrance in a separate, well-lit room so accidental cave openings do not compromise the living space.

#### Nightly Inspection

> A shelter is finished when it is predictable: every entrance is visible, every work station has a place, and the route home can be followed in the dark.

Before sleeping, check the door, roof, nearby shadows, and furnace fuel. This routine takes seconds and prevents many avoidable surprises. When the base is stable, use the resource storage habits here to support the safer mining patterns in [[mining-and-ore-safety|mining and ore safety]].
