# Food and Farming

Food changes survival from a series of emergencies into a repeatable routine. Early on, cooked meat is convenient because animals are easy to find and a furnace is simple to operate. It should not remain the only plan, though. A renewable crop field and a small breeding group reduce the pressure to travel whenever the hunger bar drops.

## A Useful Starter Farm

Place farmland beside a water source and keep the plot close to the base. A single water block can hydrate farmland across a small square area, allowing a compact field to produce wheat, carrots, potatoes, or beetroot. Fence the edges if animals or wandering players can trample crops. Replant after every harvest and keep a chest nearby for seeds and spare food.

### Choosing What to Grow

Wheat is valuable because it feeds the player and breeds cows and sheep. Carrots and potatoes are convenient direct food once a village or a lucky drop provides them. Sugar cane deserves its own row because paper supports books and therefore the enchanting setup described in [[enchanting-and-progression|enchanting and progression]]. Keep a few mature crops untouched when possible; a completely harvested field is less useful than a steady partial harvest.

#### A Sustainable Loop

1. Harvest only mature plants.
2. Replant immediately and store the surplus.
3. Breed animals only when there is enough feed for the next trip.

The first-night routine in [[first-night-plan|first-night plan]] helps gather the initial seeds and meat. Later, a lit path from the farm to the shelter makes maintenance safe after sunset. Once food is reliable, carry a full stack of cooked meals before following the hazard checks in [[mining-and-ore-safety|mining and ore safety]].
