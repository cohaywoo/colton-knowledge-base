# Mining and Ore Safety

Mining becomes profitable when preparation is treated as part of the trip. Before leaving the shelter, carry a pickaxe, a weapon, torches, food, building blocks, and a few spare tools. Bring a crafting table if the route will be long. Empty inventory space is also a resource: a player who fills every slot with cobblestone has no room for useful ore or emergency blocks.

![A Minecraft diamond ore block exposed in stone](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5c/Minecraft_diamond_ore.png)

## Make the Route Recoverable

Place torches consistently on one side of a tunnel, then keep them on the opposite side while returning. This simple rule makes a branching mine readable without signs. Mark the entrance with a distinct block and record its coordinates when the location matters. Never dig straight down into an unknown block; staircases and controlled shafts expose hazards before the player drops into them.

### Deal With Cave Hazards

Water and lava can push a player into danger, so keep a bucket and solid blocks ready. When a lava pocket appears, block the source before mining nearby. Gravel and sand can collapse into an open tunnel, while hostile mobs can approach from unlit side passages. Light each branch before collecting ore and listen for nearby mobs rather than rushing toward the sound.

#### Return Before It Is Urgent

> The safest mining trip ends while the player still has food, tools, and a clear route home.

Return when the inventory is nearly full, the pickaxe is low, or the route has become confusing. Store ore in the organized shelter from [[shelter-and-lighting|shelter and lighting]] before starting another trip. Coal, iron, diamonds, and redstone are not equally urgent, but all become more useful when the renewable food system in [[food-and-farming|food and farming]] removes the need to rush.
